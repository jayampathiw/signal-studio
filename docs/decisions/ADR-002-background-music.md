# ADR-002: Background music — one library, three ways to fill it

**Status: accepted 2026-10-15.** Layers 1 and 2 are planned and approved. Layer 3 (automated AI
generation) is **deferred**: recorded so it isn't lost, not started. Tracked as Track A in
`docs/refactor/refactor-plan.md` §10.

## Context

`clips-overlay` and `compilation` videos currently end in silence. The render side already
supports a music bed: `Timeline.music` is mixed at `gain_db` (default −18 dB), ducked under the
voiceover and faded out over 1.5 s (`ClipsOverlay.tsx`'s `MusicBed`). Nothing ever supplies a file,
though:

- `project.yaml`'s `brand.musicBeds` is `[]` and nothing reads it as a default.
- `manifest.audio.music.file` is passed straight to the renderer as a path. A plain filename is
  read from the worker's own disk, where no music exists. Only an `http(s)` URL works today.

The other templates are out of scope: `stills-kenburns` has its own `musicPlan` design,
`shorts-916`/`case-file` have fixed audio designs from their goldens, and `carousel` has no audio.

What we already have:

- **The audio kit**: 9 instrumental beds (Pixabay) + 14 SFX (Freesound), downloaded for the old
  longform pipeline. Local copy in the `main` worktree at `content/audio-kit/` (gitignored audio,
  84 MB). Every file is also on R2 under `audio-kit/` (verified reachable 2026-10-15). The owner
  checked the licence terms of every track before downloading. The beds' moods (somber, tension,
  tragic_loss…) were picked for football documentaries, so each needs a listen before it's
  enabled for AssembleX.
- **API keys** in `.env`: `FREESOUND_CLIENT_ID`/`FREESOUND_CLIENT_SECRET` (set) and
  `PIXABAY_API_KEY` (set, but Pixabay's API covers images and video only, not music).

## Decision

**The engine only ever reads from one music library. Three layers fill it.** No track is fetched or
generated while a video is rendering, so renders never wait on, or fail because of, a music source.

| Layer                | How tracks get in                                                                  | Sources                                                                                                                      | Status               |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------- |
| **1. Local library** | By hand: download or generate, drop in the local folder, `ss music sync`           | The 9 audio-kit beds; Pixabay Music, YouTube Audio Library, Meta Sound Collection; music generated by hand in **Flow Music** | **Planned — first**  |
| **2. API**           | Script: `ss music fetch` searches an API and imports matches                       | Freesound (CC0 only), key already in `.env`; Jamendo (CC-BY only) later, needs a key                                         | **Planned — second** |
| **3. AI generation** | Automated: monthly batch on a GPU that starts, generates, uploads, then shuts down | ACE-Step (open weights, commercial use allowed) on a cloud GPU; Lyria (Gemini API, about $0.08/track) as an alternative      | **Deferred**         |

### Ruled out

- **Popular or copyrighted songs mixed into the file.** Meta's Rights Manager matches every upload.
  At an automated posting rate, matches pile up against one page (France Aujourd'hui) and can mute,
  block or unpublish it. Meta's in-app licence only covers sounds added in the app by a person.
  This is supported legitimately by the `in-app` mode below.
- **Scraping Pixabay Music** (no official music API; scraping breaks its terms) and **unofficial
  Suno wrappers** (cookie-based, break Suno's terms).
- **Automating consumer AI subscriptions** (Google AI Pro, SuperGrok). They grant no API access,
  and browser automation breaks their terms. See
  `docs/research/AI subscription automation image video audio.md`.
- **An always-on GPU server** (about $110–400/month vs. about $1–5 for a monthly batch).

## Design

### Where things live

- **Local master copy**: `~/projects/personal/signal-studio/music-library/` (outside git). The
  audio-kit beds move here first, because the `main` worktree's `content/audio-kit/` is a leftover
  that a future `git pull` / cleanup could remove.
- **R2**: `music/<id>.<ext>`, uploaded by `ss music sync`. The worker downloads from here.
- **Metadata**: `projects/music/library.yaml`, committed. It's the licence record: where each track
  came from, under what terms, and what it may be used for. It's workspace content, so it moves
  with `projects/` at the P6 split.

### `library.yaml` entry

```yaml
- id: reflective-piano-01 # stable; also the R2 key stem
  file: reflective.mp3 # filename in the local folder
  source:
    pixabay # pixabay | youtube-audio-library | meta-sound-collection
    # | flow-music | freesound | jamendo | ai-batch
  licence: pixabay-content-license
  source_url: https://pixabay.com/music/… # page it came from, if known
  author: … # artist / uploader, if known
  attribution: null # credit text if the licence requires one, else null
  platforms: all # or e.g. [facebook, instagram] for Meta Sound Collection
  moods: [reflective, calm]
  duration_s: 195.9 # filled in by `ss music sync` via ffprobe
  enabled: true # false = never picked (bad fit, claim received, pending review)
  added: 2026-10-15
```

### How a job gets its track

`manifest.audio.music` gains a `mode`:

- `auto` (default): pick from the library.
- `file`: a specific library `id` (or an `http(s)` URL, which already works).
- `none`: no music.
- `in-app`: no music in the render, plus a note on the job that a person adds a trending sound in
  the Facebook/Instagram app when posting.

For `auto`, the worker:

1. Keeps tracks that are `enabled` and allowed on **every** platform the job publishes to. A job
   that also goes to YouTube never gets a Meta Sound Collection track.
2. Narrows to `manifest.audio.music.mood`; widens back to all moods if none match. **A job with no
   `mood` (and no `file`) gets no music** (owner's call, 2026-10-15): an unset mood is not a licence
   to play any track.
3. Narrows to the project's `brand.musicBeds` list of ids if it's non-empty.
4. Picks deterministically from `hash(job.id)` so retries get the same track, preferring tracks the
   project hasn't used recently, to spread rotation.
5. Records the chosen id on the job, so a retry or re-render reuses it and the dashboard can show it.
6. Downloads `music/<id>` from R2 into the job's tmp dir (same pattern as the watermark), then
   hands the local path to the existing mixing.
7. Appends the track's `attribution` to the captions when it has one.

**Empty library or nothing allowed:** the job renders without music and logs a warning. It never
fails because of music. Once layer 3 exists, this is where an AI fallback could plug in.

### Layer 2: API imports

`ss music fetch freesound --query "<terms>" --mood <mood> [--limit N]`:

- Searches with the Freesound API, **CC0 only** and a minimum duration, so it gets beds, not
  one-shots.
- Downloads each match, fills in `source_url`/`author`/`licence`, and adds it to `library.yaml`
  with **`enabled: false`**. A person listens and enables it.
- Auto-enabling can become a per-project option later, once the results have proven good enough.
- Open technical point: the Freesound API key (token auth) gives HQ MP3 previews. Full-quality
  originals need OAuth2. Previews are likely good enough for a −18 dB bed; confirm by ear.

### Layer 3: AI generation (deferred)

Recorded so it can be picked up later without redoing the research:

- **Preferred:** a monthly ACE-Step batch on a cloud GPU (about 1–2 GPU-hours, roughly $1–5/month),
  writing `source: ai-batch` entries the same way layer 2 does.
- **Alternative / fallback:** Lyria via the Gemini API (about $0.08/track, no free tier) as a
  `music` provider, called only when the library has nothing allowed for a job.
- **Before starting:** GPU access on GCP (trial accounts usually can't use GPUs, so billing would
  need upgrading), a ~$1 test batch judged by ear, and a re-check of ACE-Step's and Lyria's
  commercial licence terms.

## Consequences

- Music becomes a property of the library, not of each job. Adding sources later doesn't touch the
  engine.
- `library.yaml` doubles as the evidence trail if a copyright claim ever lands.
- The owner keeps quality control: nothing auto-imported plays until someone has listened to it.
- A small amount of manual work stays in layers 1 and 2 (adding tracks, approving API imports)
  until layer 3 is turned on.
