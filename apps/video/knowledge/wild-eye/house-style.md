The Two Proven Formulas
Formula A — Hidden Intimacy (11s Reach Reels)
Drives views. Average 4,729 views per reel across 8 reels.
What works:

Confined or intimate spaces: burrows, nests, dens, grass pockets
Single sustained moment — no cuts chasing action
Slow camera: static, push-in, or near-imperceptible pan
Eyes catching light in darkness
Photorealistic fur texture as the primary visual hook
The viewer feeling like they are trespassing into a private world

Benchmark reel: "Deep Inside the Burrow — A Wild Cavy Slowly Wakes Up"
16,102 views | 54.4% watch-through | 14 follows | +0.3x distribution
Formula B — Tension/Survival (21s Conversion Reels)
Drives follows. Average 1.47 follow conversions per 1,000 views vs 0.89 for intimacy reels.
What works:

A clear external threat implied or present
Multi-scene arc: calm → threat → resolution or unresolved ending
First-person narration adds follow-trigger ("Stay low. The sky is hunting.")
Predator shadow, hawk, storm, open ground vulnerability
Unresolved ending drives comments and rewatches

Benchmark reel: "The Sky Was Hunting Her"
6,911 views | 38.5% watch-through | 13 follows | +1x distribution | 1.88 follows/1k
The Watch-Through Paradox
High watch-through does NOT equal high reach on this page.
"One Sip of Water" — 84.8% watch-through, only 204 views (+0x distribution).
The hook (first 2 seconds / thumbnail) failed before the content got a chance.
When a reel has high watch-through but low views, the problem is the opening frame — not the script.

Image Portrait Workflow
Introduced June 2026 as a parallel content type. Posts on low-risk days (Sunday/Thursday 10:00).
Format rules:

Extreme close-up, single subject
One dramatic light source — amber shaft, predawn blue, golden hour rim
Photorealistic fur/whisker/eye detail
Vertical framing (9:16)
No text overlay

Description rules for portraits (different from Reels):

2–3 sentences maximum
End with a direct question to drive comments
Fewer hashtags: 6–8 only (Facebook image algorithm is less hashtag-driven than Reels)
Post at 10:00 — midday browsing performs better for images than evening

Example portrait briefs:

Sleeping pup macro — extreme close-up, single tiny paw extended, amber burrow light
Alert ears at golden hour — side profile, both ears erect, individual hairs translucent at edges
Dewy grass at dawn — macro of whisker tip or eye with dew drop reflection
Burrow threshold — cavy face half-in/half-out of entrance, split warm/cool light

Voice and Narration Rules
Narration is optional. When used:

First-person animal POV only — never third-person narrator
Whispered delivery implied — write for a voice that barely exists
Sparse: one or two lines maximum per video
Never explanatory — adds emotional weight, never describes what viewer can see
Present tense only

Proven narration lines (use as register benchmark):

"Don't move. Don't breathe. Don't exist."
"Stay low. Stay still. The sky is hunting."
"She has not eaten in two days. Tomorrow, perhaps."
"The sky breaks. The grass bends. The small ones wait."
"One body. Two hearts. The plains are too vast to face alone."
"She has two seconds. That is all."

Audio Design Rules
Every script must specify audio cues. Hierarchy:

Close-mic animal sounds first — breathing, chewing, movement, grooming
Environmental layer second — wind direction, grass movement, water, soil settling
Distant world third — bird calls, other species, weather
Score/drone last, sparingly — barely audible tension pulse where scene earns it

Never write "ambient background sounds." Always be specific:
"Layered soft breathing of four animals slightly out of sync" — not "quiet sounds."
